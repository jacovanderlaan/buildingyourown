Generate the year in review from your own files

A year of calendar entries and a photo folder in, a month-by-month review page out — including a list of what is missing.

Based on: One cockpit instead of fifteen apps · Level: intermediate · Time: 45 minutes live, or one Build Day afternoon

An annual review fails on its first step: digging up what happened. The data to do that already exists — in your calendar, your photos and your invoices. This sample turns that digging into one script you can run every December.

Two versions

  • Personal: calendar export + photo folder → trips, busy months, places, people you met.
  • Business: calendar export + invoice list + project list → customers per month, revenue per quarter, projects started and finished. This is the annual business review most small companies skip because nobody has the evening to compile it.

Steps

  1. Export one year of calendar events (.ics or CSV). Bring a photo folder or an invoice list as the second source.
  2. Decide which fields count: a trip has a start and an end date, an invoice has a customer and a date. Write these down as three or four rules before any code exists.
  3. Ask the AI to build a small script that reads both sources and writes one page: totals for the year, then a table with one row per month.
  4. Add a section What is missing: months with far fewer entries than the median, items without a date. This list is the most useful output.
  5. Leave two sections empty for a human to write: what I am grateful for or proud of, and what I would not do again. The script must keep these when you run it again.

What you take home

A page you can regenerate every year, the field rules written down, and an honest list of where your own records are thin. In Jaco's own run, the missing-list showed that the photos of a 23-day trip had never reached the photo index, and that 10 of the 11 trip notes for 2024 had no dates.
