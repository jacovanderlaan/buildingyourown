Build Your Own Planner

Your quarter as epics and actions with a state, and a weekly review that keeps it current.

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Memory · Rules · Dashboards

An epic is a larger piece of work with a goal and an end date; actions are the steps under it. When both are files in your Memory, your AI can tell you what is open, what is waiting and what you said yes to.

You leave with

  • Your current quarter as three to five epics, each with a goal and an end date.
  • Every open action as a small note with a state (open, waiting, done) and the epic it belongs to.
  • One overview of what is open per epic, and what is waiting on someone else.
  • A weekly review of 20 minutes, written as a checklist and done once during the session.

Ideas to try

  • A bike shop plans the spring season as one epic: stock ordered, repair slots opened, staff rota ready.
  • A builder turns every renovation into an epic with actions per trade, and sees on Friday what blocks Monday.
  • A yoga studio plans the new timetable as an epic, with actions to tell members, update the website and brief the teachers.
  • A wedding caterer gives each event its own epic on the calendar, with the tastings and orders as actions under it.
  • A one-person consultant keeps the quarter at three epics, and says no to a fourth unless one is dropped.

Your turn

What must be done by the end of this quarter that is not written down anywhere yet?

What we do in the session

  1. 20 min — Write down everything open, from every list you have.
  2. 30 min — Group it into three to five epics with an end date. Park the rest.
  3. 40 min — Turn the actions into notes with a state and an epic. The AI does the typing; you check every one.
  4. 30 min — Build the overview: open per epic, and everything that is waiting.
  5. 20 min — Do your first weekly review with the checklist.

What you bring

  • Your to-do lists, wherever they live: paper, mail, an app.
  • Your calendar for the rest of the quarter.

Tools

Obsidian for the notes and the overview; Claude to turn your lists into notes. Optional: the calendar you already use, for the end dates.

Example from my own system

In my own system every action, idea and incident is a small file with a status field. A script collects them into one table, and one app shows them on my phone: 699 tracked items, in one place instead of a list per tool. Larger work is an epic, filed in the quarter it starts in, so the plan and the calendar are the same structure. See One cockpit instead of fifteen apps.

Pitfalls

  • Too many epics. Five open epics is a plan; twelve is a wish list. Park the rest and pick them up next quarter.
  • Free-text states. Done, finished, closed and ok mean the same to you and four different things to a script. Pick three or four states and stick to them.
  • Skipping the review. Without the weekly review, every planner goes back to being a pile.

Works best after

Build Your Own Memory, where the notes live; Build Your Own Rules, for the states and what each one means; and Build Your Own Dashboards, for the overview.

Concepts behind it

  • The Calendar Is the Spine — One time-axis is the backbone of a second brain — everything dated hangs off it.
  • The Experiment Loop — Play with a hypothesis: hypothesize → experiment → learn → decide. The mechanism that turns tinkering into a proven block.
  • Deliberate Simplicity — Make each part as plain as it can be without losing what matters — reuse only the fraction you actually need, and strip the rest.

Use cases

One cockpit instead of fifteen appsActions, ideas, incidents, finance, documents and photos — all as files with status fields, all readable in one app on the phone.

Resources

  • Getting Things Done — The original inbox discipline: everything gets captured, clarified and given one next action. Email triage starts here.
  • Systems, not goals — A goal is reached and then leaves you without a next move; a system keeps producing.
  • Tiny Experiments — Treat a change as an experiment with a fixed duration instead of a goal. The same move is what makes a Build Day work: one small build, then decide.
