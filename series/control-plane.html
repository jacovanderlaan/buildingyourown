Build Your Own Control Plane

One overview of everything you run, with a view for you and a view for your team on the same data.

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Memory · Planner · Dashboards · Apps

Every tool you use brings its own dashboard, and nobody looks at fifteen dashboards. The Control Plane is the one page you open first, and it works because the levels underneath gave your data one shape.

You leave with

  • One overview page that collects your open actions, issues and key lists from your own files into one table.
  • Two views on that same table: yours with everything, and a team view where private items do not appear, tested by count before you share it.
  • A visibility field on every item (private, team) and a count that proves the team view leaves out what it should.
  • A short morning routine: what you look at, in which order, and what you act on.

Ideas to try

  • A bakery owner sees today's orders, the staff schedule and open supplier issues on one page; the staff see the schedule and the orders, not the margins.
  • An installation company's planner sees jobs, open quotes and complaints; each fitter sees only today's jobs and addresses.
  • A therapy practice owner sees caseload, waiting list and unpaid invoices; each therapist sees their own clients.
  • A small hotel's front desk sees arrivals and room status; the owner sees the same page plus revenue per night.
  • A one-person business keeps its own full view and gives the bookkeeper a view with only invoices and receipts.

Your turn

Which three lists do you check every week, and who else would need to see one of them?

What we do in the session

  1. 30 min — List what you check every week, where it lives now, and who else needs to see it.
  2. 30 min — Give those items the same fields: a small file with a status, an owner and a visibility field.
  3. 60 min — Build the overview page on one table that a script fills from those files.
  4. 30 min — Add the team view as a filter on the visibility field, not as a second copy of the data.
  5. 30 min — Open the team view and count. Every private item must be missing, and you check that by number, not by eye.

What you bring

  • Your laptop and the lists you check each week: actions, open issues, orders, a pipeline of quotes.
  • One person you would show the team view to, and what they should not see.

Tools

Claude Code for the page; your Memory and the planner from the Planner level as the data; DuckDB or a spreadsheet for the one table.

Example from my own system

Every action, idea and incident in my system is a small file with a status field. A script collects them into one table, and one app with one start page shows them next to finance, documents and trips, on my phone: 699 tracked items. The 16 cockpits behind it are tabs on that same table, not 16 dashboards. Most screens were generated by AI; the work was in giving the data one shape. My own version has one view, mine. A team view starts with access control. See One cockpit instead of fifteen apps.

Pitfalls

  • Building the overview before the data has one shape. Fifteen sources with fifteen layouts give you fifteen widgets on one page, and you still read them one by one.
  • A team view made by copying. Two copies drift apart: one of my own copies had stopped updating for 20 months before I noticed. Filter one table instead.
  • Trusting the filter without counting. Hiding private items is a rule, and a rule needs a check. Before a team member sees the page, add Safeguards: access per person and a tested backup.

Works best after

Memory, Planner, Dashboards and Apps. The Control Plane combines what those levels produce; on its own it is an empty page.

Concepts behind it

  • Company Brain — The org-scale second brain: shared, structured, permission-aware knowledge AI can rely on. Wiring tools together isn't it — access is not memory.
  • Escalate by Exception — Route the easy work to the cheap path and reserve the expensive one for what actually needs it. Power by exception, not by default.
  • Deliberate Simplicity — Make each part as plain as it can be without losing what matters — reuse only the fraction you actually need, and strip the rest.
  • Smart Teams — A data-team operating model where structure carries the context, so the synchronous meeting shrinks to the merge of pre-prepared, AI-validated material.
