Build Your Own Dashboards

The few lists and numbers you check every week, on one screen, from your own files.

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Memory · Pipelines · Insights

Everyone wants the dashboard first. It only works when the data underneath has one shape, so most of this level is about that shape, and the screen comes quickly after.

You leave with

  • One dashboard or Kanban board on your own files, showing the three lists or numbers you check every week.
  • Every tile linked to the file or table it reads from, so one click shows the source.
  • One status field shared by everything on the board, with a short, fixed list of values.
  • A version you can open on your phone.

Ideas to try

  • A garden center has one board of plant orders per supplier: ordered, delivered, paid.
  • A cleaning company sees on one screen which sites were done this week, which were skipped and why.
  • A home-care team works from a Kanban board of client requests (new, planned, done), with the oldest open request at the top.
  • A guesthouse shows tonight's arrivals, open payments and rooms still to clean on a tablet at the desk.
  • A freelance developer keeps one board with quotes out, work in progress and invoices unpaid.

Your turn

Which three lists do you open every Monday morning, in three different places?

What we do in the session

  1. 20 min — Pick the three lists, and decide which decision each one helps you make.
  2. 30 min — Give them one shape: the same status field with the same few values.
  3. 50 min — Build the board with the AI, on your own files: a board view in Obsidian, or one page over a small database.
  4. 30 min — Test it on your real Monday question and fix what is missing.
  5. 20 min — Open it on your phone.

What you bring

  • The three lists, as a spreadsheet, an export or notes.
  • One decision you make from them every week.

Tools

Obsidian with a board or table view, or one HTML page over DuckDB, built with Claude. Optional: a shared folder or small home server, to reach the board from your phone.

Example from my own system

Every incident in my system, a failed import, a broken page, a dispute with a supplier, is a small file with fixed fields. A script collects the incidents together with my actions and ideas into one table, and they show up on the same dashboard: 51 incidents tracked, 36 open and 15 closed. The board showed a weak spot I had not seen: fixed, resolved, closed and a few free-text variants all meant roughly the same. A board that groups by status needs a fixed list of values first. See Incident management that turns every failure into a rule and One cockpit instead of fifteen apps.

Pitfalls

  • Building the screen before the data has one shape. Most of the work is the status field, not the layout.
  • Fifteen tiles. Nobody reads fifteen. Start with three that each lead to a decision.
  • A number without a date. A tile that stopped updating looks exactly like one that is current. Show when it was last refreshed.

Works best after

Build Your Own Memory and Build Your Own Pipelines, so the board has files and data to read, and Build Your Own Insights, so you know which numbers deserve a place on it.

Concepts behind it

  • A Toy vs an Instrument — Without structure AI is a toy; with structure it becomes an instrument.
  • Escalate by Exception — Route the easy work to the cheap path and reserve the expensive one for what actually needs it. Power by exception, not by default.
  • Play with the Blocks — Like Lego — snap, try, rebuild. Tinker in your own life to learn what's possible.
