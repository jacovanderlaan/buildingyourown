Data · Level 3

Build Your Own Sources

Every place your data lives, with an owner and a date, and the first one brought home.

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Memory · Models

Your business data is spread over a webshop, a bank, a mailbox, an accounting package and a few spreadsheets. Each of them is a source. Before an AI can answer questions across them, you need to know which sources exist, who keeps them, and how old the copy is.

You leave with

  • A source list: every system and file your business data lives in, with its owner, its format, how you get an export, and how often it changes.
  • A freshness date on every source: when you last had a copy, and how old is too old.
  • One source imported for real, for example a CSV export loaded into a small database or a spreadsheet you can query.
  • The original export file kept next to it, so the copy can always be rebuilt.

Ideas to try

  • A web shop lists its order platform, payment provider and stock sheet, and finds that stock lives in two places with two owners.
  • A plumbing company brings home the job list from its planning app as a weekly export, instead of reading it on screen.
  • A physiotherapy practice lists where appointments, invoices and referral letters live, and sees which one nobody can export.
  • A café exports its till data per month and finally sees sales per hour next to the staff roster.
  • A one-person bookkeeper lists the sources of each client and the day each one usually arrives.

Your turn

Which data do you only ever see inside someone else's app? Bring one export of it, or find out on the day that you cannot get one.

What we do in the session

  1. 40 min — List every source: systems, mailboxes, spreadsheets, paper. For each one: who owns it, which format, how you get it out.
  2. 20 min — Give each source a freshness rule: daily, weekly, monthly, and when a copy counts as stale.
  3. 30 min — Pick the one source you would most like to question, and make the export together.
  4. 50 min — Load it into DuckDB or a spreadsheet with the AI. Keep the export file untouched next to it.
  5. 20 min — Ask three real questions of the copy and check one answer against the system itself.
  6. 20 min — Write a short note per source in your Memory, with the owner and the date of the copy.

What you bring

  • Logins to the two or three systems you use most, so we can make the exports on the day.
  • One export you already have: a customer list, a month of orders or a bank statement as CSV.

Tools

A spreadsheet for the source list; DuckDB (free) for the imported copy; Claude to read the export and write the import. Optional: the export functions your systems already have, which are often hidden in a settings menu.

Example from my own system

I kept a local copy of my CRM data to analyze it. It answered every question, and it had stopped updating twenty months earlier. Three different import scripts existed, and none of them had an owner. The fix took one day: one importer, one owner, and a check that fails loudly when the copy is more than seven days old. After the fix the number of people dropped by half, which was correct: the old copy carried duplicates. See The mirror that went stale and nobody noticed.

Pitfalls

  • A copy without a date. Every export starts going stale the day you make it. If you cannot see how old it is, you will trust it too long.
  • Two sources, no owner. When the same data lives in two places, decide which one is the source and who keeps it. The other one is a copy.
  • Bringing everything home at once. One source you can question this afternoon beats six half-loaded ones.

Works best after

Build Your Own Memory (level 1) and Build Your Own Models (level 2). The model tells you which source holds which entity.

Concepts behind it

  • Facts Expire — Knowledge doesn't become false, it becomes superseded. A store that only accumulates is a store that quietly gets less true.
  • Own the Layer or Rent the Feature — Every knowledge product answers two questions: who maintains it, and is it a layer of its own or a feature of something else. The answers decide what you can build on.
  • Find the Essence First — Distil the essence before the detail — in interviews, grasp the data and the bigger picture first, then drill down; and quickly identify what actually works in the giants who came before us.

Resources

  • DuckDB — A database in a single file, no server. Ideal as a rebuildable mirror of files you own.
  • Verify on fresh output — Verify on fresh output, not on what the script printed. Check the artefact, not the log line claiming it is there.
