Build Your Own Interfaces

Ways to ask your system and tell it things away from the laptop: on your phone, by voice, with one button.

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Memory · Integrations · Skills

Integrations connect your AI to other systems. Interfaces connect you to it, at the moment you need an answer, which is rarely when you sit behind a screen.

You leave with

  • One way to ask your Memory a question from your phone, with an answer that names the note it came from.
  • One way to put something in from your phone: a photo, a voice note or a short form that lands in the right folder with the right header.
  • A short list of what the interface may do and what it may not, for example "may read, may not send mail".
  • Three real questions you asked it on the move, with the answers it gave.

Ideas to try

  • An installer on a roof asks "what did we quote this customer last year?" and hears the amount and the date of the quote.
  • A restaurant chef says "we are out of the lamb" into the phone, and the note for tonight's menu is updated.
  • A home care worker logs a visit with three taps, and the client note gets the date, the time and what was done.
  • A shop owner presses one button on the phone when a product runs out, and it goes on the order list.
  • A one-person bookkeeper photographs a receipt and finds it filed by day before the coffee is finished.

Your turn

Where are you when you most need an answer from your own records, and what do you have in your hands at that moment?

What we do in the session

  1. 20 min — Pick the moment: where you are, what you need to know or record, and how much time you have.
  2. 30 min — Decide what goes in and what comes out. Write it as two short lists, plus the things the interface must never do.
  3. 70 min — Build the path from your phone to your Memory: a shortcut, a chat bot or a home screen page, connected through what you set up at the Integrations level.
  4. 30 min — Make every answer name its source, and make the interface say so when it does not know.
  5. 30 min — Test it away from the table, standing up, with one hand. Note what was slow or unclear.

What you bring

  • Your phone, your laptop and a Memory with real notes in it.
  • Three questions you were asked last week that you could not answer on the spot.

Tools

Claude with a connector to your notes; your phone's own shortcuts app or a messaging app; Syncthing or a shared folder to get files from the phone to the right place. Optional: a smart speaker for voice.

Example from my own system

I built a voice assistant on my own home server that answers questions from my Memory. The test I hold it to: asked about an earlier decision, it gave two sentences with the date and the decision record it came from, and then said plainly which part the notes did not cover. It is a prototype. Speech goes in and out, and the answers cite their sources, but the speaker still needs one setting before it listens on its own.

Pitfalls

  • An answer without a source. On a phone you will not check it, so the interface must show where an answer comes from. A voice that guesses is worse than one that stays quiet.
  • Testing the parts, not the path. My speaker stayed dark because it was set to "use the preferred assistant" and no assistant was marked as preferred. Each setting was valid on its own. Test end to end.
  • Letting it act without asking. Reading is safe to start with. Sending, deleting or paying needs a confirmation step.

Works best after

Memory, Integrations and Skills. The interface is the front door; what it opens onto has to be ordered, connected and able to do the task first.

Concepts behind it

  • A Confident Error Has No Tell — A hallucination arrives in exactly the same shape as a correct answer. That is the whole problem — and why fluency is not evidence.
  • Company Brain — The org-scale second brain: shared, structured, permission-aware knowledge AI can rely on. Wiring tools together isn't it — access is not memory.
  • Design Thinking — Approach modeling as design: understand the real need, explore options, and experiment fast — and with AI you can now try new methods, build applications and test them at a speed that was impossible before.
