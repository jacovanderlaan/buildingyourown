Build Your Own Integrations

the connectors that give your AI access

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Memory · Sources

The connectors that give your AI access. An integration links one system to another: your AI to your calendar, your mail, your CRM or your bookkeeping. That is system to system. The ways you, as a person, talk to your system, such as a phone or a button, come later, at the Interfaces level. With one connector in place, the AI answers from your real data instead of from what you paste into the chat.

You leave with

  • Your AI connected to one system you use every day (mail, calendar, CRM or bookkeeping) through a connector.
  • Read-only access first, written down: what the AI may read, and what it may not change.
  • Three real questions answered through the connector, each checked against the system itself.
  • A short note in your Memory: which connector, which account, what it may do, and how to switch it off.

Ideas to try

  • A shoe shop connects its AI to the web shop's order system and asks which sizes were returned most this season.
  • A landscaping company connects its calendar and asks which jobs next week still have no confirmed address.
  • A speech therapist connects her mailbox and asks which referrers have not sent anyone since spring.
  • A bed and breakfast connects its booking calendar and asks which weeks in May are still empty.
  • A one-person consultant connects the bookkeeping and asks which invoices are open longer than 30 days.

Your turn

Which system do you open just to look up one thing? Bring the three questions you look up most.

What we do in the session

  1. 20 min — Choose the system and write down the three questions you want the AI to answer from it.
  2. 30 min — Look at what exists: many systems already have a connector you can use. Build only when none fits.
  3. 50 min — Connect it, read-only, to your own account. We test with real data, not a demo.
  4. 40 min — Ask the three questions and check every answer against the system itself. Note where the AI was wrong.
  5. 20 min — Decide what the AI may do beyond reading, if anything, and write that rule down.
  6. 20 min — Write the note: which connector, what it may do, and how to switch it off.

What you bring

  • A login to the one system you chose, with the right to create an access key or connect an app.
  • The three questions, and the answers you already know, so we can check the AI against them.

Tools

Claude (desktop or Claude Code) with connectors (MCP) for common systems such as mail and calendar; a small custom connector when none exists. Optional: a local copy of the data from the Sources level, which you can connect instead of the live system.

Example from my own system

My bookkeeping stays in the accounting package; that is the official record and nothing is booked twice. A mirror of the export, 11,587 bookings over 155 ledger accounts, sits in a local database with a small connector on top. I ask it questions in plain language: profit and loss per quarter, open invoices, revenue per customer. The questions the package makes hard became one sentence. See Keep the accounting package, own the booking rules. My mail archive works the same way (Your mail archive as a database you own).

Pitfalls

  • Write access on day one. Start with read-only. An AI that can change your bookkeeping needs rules first; that is a later level.
  • Believing the first answer. A connector gives the AI data, not judgment. Check three answers against the system before you trust the fourth.
  • One connector per question. Connect the system once and ask it many things, instead of building a new link for every report.

Works best after

Build Your Own Memory (level 1) and Build Your Own Sources (level 3). The source list tells you which system is worth connecting first.

Concepts behind it

  • Rent the AI; Own the Structure — Models are swappable commodities; your structure is the durable asset.
  • The Model Proposes, the Harness Disposes — Give the model no direct external actions. It reasons and suggests; the surrounding layer validates, executes and records — that split is what makes an agent governable.
  • Do Not Reinvent the Wheel — The new way of modeling stands on the shoulders of giants — distil the essentials from decades of data-modeling work and keep what works, rather than starting from scratch.
