Build Your Own Insights

Questions about your business answered from your own data, each with the number and where it comes from.

Duration: half a day · Builds on: Models · Pipelines · Checks

This is where the layers underneath pay off: the structure, the data and the checks turn a question into an answer you can trust and repeat.

You leave with

  • Three questions about your business answered from your own data, each with the number and the table it comes from.
  • The query behind each answer, saved, so you can run it again next month.
  • One answer checked by hand against the source, so you know the method holds.
  • A list of the questions you could not answer yet, and which data is missing for them.

Ideas to try

  • A flower shop finds the customers who ordered for a birthday last year and have not ordered this year.
  • An electrician sees which kind of job earns the most per hour once travel time is counted.
  • A dental practice counts missed appointments per weekday, and moves the reminder to the day before the worst one.
  • A café puts takings per hour next to the staff rota and sees which shift is overstaffed.
  • A freelance designer learns how much of the year's revenue came from the top three clients.

Your turn

Which number would you like to know about your business, that nobody can give you without a day of spreadsheet work?

What we do in the session

  1. 20 min — Choose three questions, and write down the answer you expect for each.
  2. 40 min — Load your exports into one small database.
  3. 50 min — Answer each question. The AI writes the query; you read it and run it.
  4. 30 min — Check one answer by hand against the original export.
  5. 20 min — Write each answer down with the number, the source and the date.

What you bring

  • One or two exports from your own systems (sales, invoices, bookings, appointments), as CSV or Excel.
  • Three questions, with your guess for each answer.

Tools

DuckDB (free) for the data; Claude to write the queries; a spreadsheet for the hand check. Optional: Obsidian, to keep the answers as notes in your Memory.

Example from my own system

A CRM sells one feature above all: who you are losing touch with. I rebuilt it from my own mail, messages and calendar: 493,492 interactions in one table, scored on six factors the CRM vendor itself publishes, with my own weights. That gives 223 scored relationships. One factor did more than I expected: one-to-one mail versus bulk mail separates real relationships from newsletters, without a blocklist. See A relationship score from your own mail and messages.

Pitfalls

  • An answer without its source. A wrong number arrives in exactly the same shape as a right one. If it cannot point at its table, do not use it.
  • Asking the AI for the answer instead of the query. Let it write the query, read what it does, and run it yourself.
  • Skipping the checks. If a customer appears three times, your top-ten list is wrong. Clean the data before you ask it anything.

Works best after

Build Your Own Models, Build Your Own Pipelines and Build Your Own Checks: a clear model, data that arrives by itself, and checks that tell you the data can be trusted.

Concepts behind it

  • Reality Check — Test the model and the plan against the real world — the actual data, deliveries and behavior — not against the assumption you started with.
  • A Confident Error Has No Tell — A hallucination arrives in exactly the same shape as a correct answer. That is the whole problem — and why fluency is not evidence.
  • Own the Layer or Rent the Feature — Every knowledge product answers two questions: who maintains it, and is it a layer of its own or a feature of something else. The answers decide what you can build on.
