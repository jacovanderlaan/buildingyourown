Accounting and bank transactions
in progress

Every booking linked to its scan

Scans carry date, vendor and amount in a small metadata file; bookings come from the accounting export. A match on date and amount links them, and two short lists show what is missing on either side.

Replaces
searching for the receipt when the accountant asks
Built with
day folders with YAML sidecars, a bookkeeping export mirrored in DuckDB, a matching script
In numbers
11,587 bookings mirrored · 4,289 receipts with sidecars

The situation

At year end, or when the accountant asks, someone hunts for the receipt behind a booking. Or finds a receipt and cannot tell whether it was ever booked.

What I built

  • Every scan lives in the folder of its date, with a sidecar holding date, vendor, amount and category.
  • The bookkeeping export is mirrored in a local database: date, ledger account, description, amount.
  • A matching step joins the two on date and amount, with the vendor name as a tie-breaker, and writes the link into the sidecar.
  • Two lists come out: bookings without a scan, and scans without a booking. Those lists are the to-do, not the thousands that matched.

Status

Day folders, sidecars and the bookkeeping mirror are built and in daily use. The matching runs as a step, not yet as a screen; the bookkeeping package itself stays the official record.

What I learned on the way

Check the instrument before the result. A field that says whether a booking has an attachment came out yes for every one of 11,587 bookings — which is not believable, so the field is not used until it is understood. A number that looks perfect is the first one to distrust.

The structure underneath

A link is data. Once the scan knows its booking and the booking knows its scan, an audit question becomes a lookup.

Start on a Build Day

Bring one month of bookings (an export) and the scans of that month. Match them on date and amount, and look at what is left on both lists.

Concepts behind it

  • The Calendar Is the Spine — One time-axis is the backbone of a second brain — everything dated hangs off it.
  • Reality Check — Test the model and the plan against the real world — the actual data, deliveries and behavior — not against the assumption you started with.
  • Rules You Can Verify — '2-space indent' is a rule; 'format code properly' is a wish. A rule the system can't check is decoration.
