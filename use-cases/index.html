Use cases

Building your own business OS

Accounting, email, CRM, documents, digital assets — the components every small company runs on. These are the ones I built myself instead of buying, with AI writing most of the code and the structure underneath doing most of the work. Every number is real.

Accounting and bank transactions

Keep the accounting package, own the booking rulesThe official books stay in the accounting package. The knowledge of how things are booked moves out of people's heads into a readable rule file, and a mirror of the books makes them queryable.A household and business ledger you ownBank exports from several banks and a credit card land in one database, every row tagged personal or business and categorised by readable rules. The finance app becomes a view, not a vault.Every booking linked to its scanScans carry date, vendor and amount in a small metadata file; bookings come from the accounting export. A match on date and amount links them, and two short lists show what is missing on either side.in progressEvery receipt filed on the day it happenedReceipts, invoices and statements are filed as PDFs in the folder of the date they belong to, each with a small metadata file next to it. Matching them to bank lines becomes a join on date and amount.

Email and inbox management

From 95 mails to the one that needs youExisting mail filters and a set of decisions per sender become one classification, and a daily summary shows only what needs a human.A purchase history from order confirmationsEvery web shop sends an order confirmation. Read them all into one table and you have a purchase history across every shop — warranty dates included.in progressYour mail archive as a database you ownMail is the biggest record a small company has of its customers, suppliers and agreements — and it lives in someone else's search box. A local, queryable copy turns it into data.

CRM, contacts and relationships

Replacing a CRM before the renewal dateInstead of switching to another CRM, I model the work itself — people, organisations and recursive activities — and grow my own cockpit towards a fixed exit date.in progressOne source, three publications: birthdaysA small case with a big principle: one file is the source, and the calendar, the contact list and a web page are publications generated from it.A relationship score from your own mail and messagesA CRM's most valuable feature — who you are losing touch with — rebuilt from data you already own, with the same published factors and your own weights.The mirror that went stale and nobody noticedA copy of my CRM data looked fine for twenty months. It had stopped updating. A lesson in why every copy of data needs a date and an alarm.

Document management

Document intake that keeps its own receiptsFeeding thousands of documents into a document system, with a ledger that proves what arrived, avoids duplicates, and re-reads the badly recognised ones.From phone scan to the right folder, named and indexedA scan on the phone arrives in an inbox folder; one instruction routes it to its proper place, with a proper name and a metadata file.

Digital asset management

Leaving an asset manager that copies everythingAn asset manager that must copy every file into its own library doubles your storage and owns your tags. The way out: tags into files next to the photos, and viewers that only read.A photo pipeline that knows what is privateEvery new photo is filed by day and activity, gets a metadata file, and is classified private, shared or never-share by rules on its folder — before anything leaves the house.

The rest of the business OS

One cockpit instead of fifteen appsActions, ideas, incidents, finance, documents and photos — all as files with status fields, all readable in one app on the phone.
