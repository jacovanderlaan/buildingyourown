Accounting and bank transactions

A household and business ledger you own

Bank exports from several banks and a credit card land in one database, every row tagged personal or business and categorised by readable rules. The finance app becomes a view, not a vault.

Replaces
a home-finance app (MijnGeldzaken)
Built with
Python, DuckDB, one static HTML app
In numbers
14,624 transactions since 2002 · 32 accounts

The situation

Most people keep their money overview in an app they rent: a budgeting tool, the bank's own dashboard, a spreadsheet that breaks every January. The data lives in the app. When the app changes or stops, the history goes with it.

What I built

A small importer reads the export files of every bank and card I use and writes them into one database: accounts, transactions, and for every row who it belongs to and whether it is personal or business. A rule file (plain YAML, one line per pattern) decides the category. One screen in my own app shows the result per month, per category, per trip.

The structure underneath

  • One table of accounts, one of transactions — the same shape for every bank.
  • Rules as data: "this pattern → this category" lives in a file anyone can read and change, not inside a black box.
  • The export file is kept. The database can be rebuilt from it at any time.

What went wrong, and what it taught me

Bank links (PSD2) expire quietly. Five of six accounts at one bank had not synced for months before I noticed. The fix is not a better link but a warning: consent expires in 12 days. Build the alarm before you trust the automation.

Start on a Build Day

Bring one month of exported transactions (anonymised) and five rules you apply in your head. By the afternoon you have a categorised overview and a rule file you can extend.

Concepts behind it

  • Rules as Data — Constraints and logic expressed as data, not buried in code — rules say what must hold, versioned and enforceable, so quality and governance travel with the model.
  • Rules You Can Verify — '2-space indent' is a rule; 'format code properly' is a wish. A rule the system can't check is decoration.
  • Silence Is Not Absence of Failure — No complaints does not mean no problems. It usually means people adapted to the errors and stopped mentioning them.
