Digital asset management

Leaving an asset manager that copies everything

An asset manager that must copy every file into its own library doubles your storage and owns your tags. The way out: tags into files next to the photos, and viewers that only read.

Replaces
a desktop asset manager (Eagle)
Built with
sidecar files, a self-hosted photo viewer (Immich) reading the files read-only
In numbers
1.1 TB library removed · 80,752 duplicated items in a second library

The situation

Many digital asset managers import files into their own library. Your tags, ratings and folders live inside it. Leave the app, and you lose the work.

What I did

  • Exported every tag into a database and then into sidecar files next to the photos (275,223 images, 86,945 with tags).
  • Removed the 1.1 TB library.
  • Switched to a viewer that references the original folders read-only instead of copying them.
  • When I change something in the viewer — delete, favourite, album — it is written back to the sidecar. The viewer proposes; the file decides.

The structure underneath

Choose tools that point at your files, not tools that swallow them. The same holds for documents, product images and brand assets in a company.

Start on a Build Day

Bring a folder of product or project images. Define five tags, write them to sidecars, and generate a simple gallery from them.

Concepts behind it

  • Own the Layer or Rent the Feature — Every knowledge product answers two questions: who maintains it, and is it a layer of its own or a feature of something else. The answers decide what you can build on.
  • Document Vault — Plain files in a deliberate shape that both you and AI can navigate.
