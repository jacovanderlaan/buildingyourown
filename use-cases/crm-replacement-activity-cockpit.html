Replacing a CRM before the renewal date

Instead of switching to another CRM, I model the work itself — people, organisations and recursive activities — and grow my own cockpit towards a fixed exit date.

Replaces
a rented CRM (Cloze), renewal July 2027
Built with
one static HTML app over DuckDB; data model after Silverston's Work Effort
In numbers
7,574 people · 4,106 companies · 992 projects mirrored

The situation

A CRM renewal is a moment of truth: pay again, move to a competitor, or stop. Moving means migrating data into someone else's model once more.

What I am building

First a mirror of the CRM (people, companies, projects, agreements) that I can query and write to, with dry-run on by default. Then my own cockpit on that mirror, designed from a teardown of what the CRM actually does well. The heart is one generic, recursive Activity entity — a project, a meeting, a deal and a to-do are all activities — taken from a classic data-modeling pattern.

Status

Mirror and write layer: built. People, organisations and project screens: built in September 2026. The exit date is fixed: before the next renewal.

The structure underneath

Model the work, not the tool. Once activities are modelled properly, the screens are cheap — AI generates them.

Start on a Build Day

Bring an export of your contacts or deals. Draw the three or four entities you really use; most people find they use a fraction of their CRM.

Concepts behind it

  • Proven Patterns — Proven shapes you reach for instead of inventing — a pattern captures a repeatable solution (Data Vault, dimensional, temporal) so you compose from known-good parts.
  • Own the Layer or Rent the Feature — Every knowledge product answers two questions: who maintains it, and is it a layer of its own or a feature of something else. The answers decide what you can build on.
  • Deliberate Simplicity — Make each part as plain as it can be without losing what matters — reuse only the fraction you actually need, and strip the rest.
