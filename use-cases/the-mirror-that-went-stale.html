The mirror that went stale and nobody noticed

A copy of my CRM data looked fine for twenty months. It had stopped updating. A lesson in why every copy of data needs a date and an alarm.

What happened

I kept a local copy of my CRM data so I could analyse it. It answered every question. It had also stopped updating twenty months earlier: three different import scripts existed, and none of them had an owner.

The fix

  • One importer, one owner.
  • A freshness check: the import fails loudly when the copy is more than seven days old.
  • After the fix the number of people dropped by half. That was correct — the old copy carried duplicates. Compare with the source, not with your previous state.

Why it matters for anything you build

Every copy you make — a spreadsheet export, a database, a dashboard — starts going stale the moment it is made. Give it a date and an alarm, or it will lie to you politely.

Concepts behind it

  • Silence Is Not Absence of Failure — No complaints does not mean no problems. It usually means people adapted to the errors and stopped mentioning them.
  • Uptime Is Not Correctness — Monitoring proves the system ran. Observability for AI has to prove the answer was right — and those are different instruments.
  • Data Observability — Continuous visibility into the health of your data pipelines — freshness, volume, schema drift, distribution and quality — so problems surface on their own, at the boundary, in time to fix them.
