CRM, contacts and relationships

A relationship score from your own mail and messages

A CRM's most valuable feature — who you are losing touch with — rebuilt from data you already own, with the same published factors and your own weights.

Replaces
the scoring inside a rented CRM (Cloze)
Built with
Python, DuckDB, your own mail and message exports
In numbers
493,492 interactions · 223 scored relationships

The situation

CRMs sell one thing above all: they tell you which relationships are going cold. To do that they need all your mail, all your messages and all your calendar — in their cloud.

What I built

A script that reads my own mail, message and calendar history into one table of interactions, and scores each relationship on six factors that the CRM vendor itself publishes: how long since contact, how often, how responsive, how personal, how fresh, how balanced. The weights are mine.

The structure underneath

The score is a formula over a table, not a secret. Change a weight, rerun, compare.

The surprise

The privacy factor — one-to-one mail versus bulk mail — separates real relationships from newsletters without any blocklist. One job board alone had sent almost thirty thousand mails; it drops out on its own.

Start on a Build Day

Bring an export of your contacts and a sample of your sent mail headers (no content needed). Build the interaction table and a first score with two factors.
