Email and inbox management

Your mail archive as a database you own

Mail is the biggest record a small company has of its customers, suppliers and agreements — and it lives in someone else's search box. A local, queryable copy turns it into data.

Replaces
searching in the mail provider's web interface
Built with
a local copy of the mailbox, Python, DuckDB
In numbers
hundreds of thousands of mails held locally · 465,416 mail interactions in the relationship score

The situation

Who did we quote last spring? When did this supplier last change its terms? The answer is somewhere in the mailbox, and the mail provider's search box is the only way in.

What I built

A local copy of the mailbox, read into a database: sender, recipients, date, subject, and whether it was one-to-one or bulk mail. Content stays in the mail files; the database holds what is needed to count, link and find.

What it made possible

  • The relationship score on this site runs on it: 465,416 mail interactions, plus messages.
  • Order confirmations become a purchase history (in progress).
  • Sender decisions for the daily triage are made once and applied to the whole archive.

The structure underneath

The mail provider is a delivery service, not your archive. Own the copy; let the provider deliver.

Concepts behind it

  • Own the Layer or Rent the Feature — Every knowledge product answers two questions: who maintains it, and is it a layer of its own or a feature of something else. The answers decide what you can build on.
  • Email Intelligence — Classify every sender signal-vs-noise; turn an inbox into a managed source.
