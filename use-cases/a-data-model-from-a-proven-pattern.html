Data models and app building blocks

A data model in an afternoon, from a proven pattern

Parties, products, orders, agreements, locations, accounts and events have been modelled well for decades. Start from those patterns, keep what you need, add your own rules, and generate the database from it.

Replaces
a blank page — or a heavy modeling tool you use a fraction of
Built with
industry patterns (Universal Data Models) as YAML templates, a generator, the MDDE metadata model
In numbers
7 core domains · a metadata model of 268 tables in 16 subject areas · 44 tests on the library

The situation

Every company thinks its data is unique. It mostly is not. Customers and suppliers are parties. Quotes and contracts are agreements. Projects, tickets and tasks are work efforts. These patterns were worked out decades ago — most famously in Len Silverston's Universal Data Models — and most small companies still start from an empty spreadsheet.

What I built

  • A library of industry patterns as data: seven core domains (party, product, order, agreement, location, account, event), each as a readable template rather than a diagram in a book.
  • My additions to every pattern: which modeling style fits (a plain normalised model, a star schema or a Data Vault), which layer it belongs in, and its business and data-quality rules. The same pattern serves three architectures.
  • A generator that takes a domain, applies naming and options, and writes entities, attributes and relationships into one metadata model — from which the database, documentation and diagrams are generated.
  • An AI step that reads existing tables and suggests which pattern they match, and which attributes are missing.

The structure underneath

The pattern carries the experience; the rules carry your business. You do not invent a customer model — you choose what you leave out.

Honest status

The library and generator are built and tested; the set of ready templates is still small, and the industry extensions are designed but not all filled in. My own CRM replacement already runs on one of these patterns: the work effort, as one recursive activity.

Start on a Build Day

Bring the three lists your business runs on (customers, orders, projects). We map them to the proven patterns, strip what you do not need, and generate a small database you can fill the same day.

Concepts behind it

  • Proven Patterns — Proven shapes you reach for instead of inventing — a pattern captures a repeatable solution (Data Vault, dimensional, temporal) so you compose from known-good parts.
  • Do Not Reinvent the Wheel — The new way of modeling stands on the shoulders of giants — distil the essentials from decades of data-modeling work and keep what works, rather than starting from scratch.
  • Metadata First — Metadata is the material Breakthrough Modeling is built from — structure, logic and meaning captured as data, so the platform and the AI can both read and run it.
  • Deliberate Simplicity — Make each part as plain as it can be without losing what matters — reuse only the fraction you actually need, and strip the rest.
