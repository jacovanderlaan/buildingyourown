Getting Things Done

The original inbox discipline: everything gets captured, clarified and given one next action. Email triage starts here.

David Allen · 2001

What the book is about

David Allen's method for getting work out of your head and into a system you trust: capture everything, clarify what each item means, organise it, review it weekly, and do the next action.

Ideas I took from it

  • Externalise or be occupied. Open loops in your head keep using attention until they land in a system.
  • Every item gets one type and one home. Next action, project, waiting for, someday, reference: that is a routing scheme, and it maps directly onto rules for an inbox.
  • The weekly review keeps the system honest. Without a regular check, every system decays back into a pile.

On a Build Day

Most inbox tools on a Build Day are GTD as code: sender rules that sort mail into a few clear types, so only what needs you reaches you.

Concepts behind it

  • Inbox Triage — Every place loose input arrives needs a fast per-item sort — process, archive, discard, defer — before anything reaches the vault.
  • Email Intelligence — Classify every sender signal-vs-noise; turn an inbox into a managed source.
  • Escalate by Exception — Route the easy work to the cheap path and reserve the expensive one for what actually needs it. Power by exception, not by default.
