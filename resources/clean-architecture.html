Clean Architecture

Keep the rules of the business apart from the tools around them. That separation is what lets you swap the AI, the database or the screen later.

Robert C. Martin · 2017

What the book is about

Robert C. Martin on how to structure software so it stays changeable. The business rules sit at the centre; the database, the screens and the frameworks are details on the outside that you can replace.

Ideas I took from it

  • Business rules at the centre, everything else is a plug-in. The rules of your company should not live inside one tool.
  • Dependencies point inward. The tools may know about your rules; your rules should not depend on the tools.
  • Keep decisions open. Good architecture lets you postpone the choice of database or vendor without paying for it later.

On a Build Day

This is why we write the rules down as a separate file first. When the AI model changes, or you swap the screen for a different one, the rules stay.

Concepts behind it

  • Rent the AI; Own the Structure — Models are swappable commodities; your structure is the durable asset.
  • Own the Layer or Rent the Feature — Every knowledge product answers two questions: who maintains it, and is it a layer of its own or a feature of something else. The answers decide what you can build on.
  • Meaning Before Mechanism — A semantic model is not a technology. The same meaning can ship as a graph, an API, a data product or a contract — decide what it means before deciding what to buy.
  • Rules as Data — Constraints and logic expressed as data, not buried in code — rules say what must hold, versioned and enforceable, so quality and governance travel with the model.
