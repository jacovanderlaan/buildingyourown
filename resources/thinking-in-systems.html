Thinking in Systems: A Primer

Stocks, flows and feedback loops. Useful for seeing why a tool that works on day one can drift by month three.

Donella H. Meadows · 2008

What the book is about

Donella Meadows' primer on systems thinking: stocks that accumulate, flows that fill and drain them, and feedback loops that make a system grow, balance or drift.

Ideas I took from it

  • Structure is the source of behaviour. Change the structure and the behaviour follows; this is the root of "structure beats magic".
  • Rules are a strong leverage point. Changing the rules of a system does more than tuning its numbers.
  • Look below the events. Reacting to every incident keeps you firefighting; the structure explains why they keep happening.

On a Build Day

A tool that works on day one can drift by month three, when an export changes or a feed stops. We design the feedback loop that tells you.

Concepts behind it

  • Structure Beats Magic — The magic isn't in the model; it's in the structure you give it.
  • Rules as Data — Constraints and logic expressed as data, not buried in code — rules say what must hold, versioned and enforceable, so quality and governance travel with the model.
  • Compounding Efficiency — Spend time, money and attention where they compound — cut the meetings, the licensed bloat and the unstructured chat that add nothing, and put the resource into structure that pays back.
  • Facts Expire — Knowledge doesn't become false, it becomes superseded. A store that only accumulates is a store that quietly gets less true.
