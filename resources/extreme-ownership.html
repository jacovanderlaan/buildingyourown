Extreme Ownership

If you build it, you own it — including the import that silently stopped running.

Jocko Willink & Leif Babin · 2015

What the book is about

Jocko Willink and Leif Babin translate their experience as Navy SEAL leaders into principles for teams and businesses. The core: the leader owns everything in their world, including the failures. No excuses, no blaming.

Ideas I took from it

  • Own the outcome, not only your part. If a tool you built gives a wrong number, it is yours to fix, whoever touched it last.
  • Keep it simple. A plan that people cannot repeat in their own words will not be executed.
  • Prioritise and execute. Under pressure, pick the most important problem, solve it, then the next.

On a Build Day

A home-made tool has no vendor to blame. That is why we build a small check that tells you when an import stopped running, so silence does not look like success.

Concepts behind it

  • Silence Is Not Absence of Failure — No complaints does not mean no problems. It usually means people adapted to the errors and stopped mentioning them.
  • Incident Modeling — Model delivery incidents as data, not loose tickets — a late feed, a failed quality check, a broken schema becomes a tracked, owned, queryable object in the model itself.
  • Uptime Is Not Correctness — Monitoring proves the system ran. Observability for AI has to prove the answer was right — and those are different instruments.
