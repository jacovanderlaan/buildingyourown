Life Design

Treat it like a design problem: prototype, test, iterate, instead of planning it once and hoping.

Jaco van der Laan

The idea

Most planning assumes you can think your way to the right answer, commit and execute. Life Design borrows from design practice instead: you do not know the answer up front, so you prototype it. You run small, cheap experiments, gather real evidence and iterate, and uncertainty becomes information instead of risk.

I wrote it for my own life. It works the same for a company's first AI tool.

On a Build Day

The day is one prototype loop: build the smallest version on real data, try it, learn, adjust. No big plan has to be right first.

Concepts behind it

  • Design Thinking — Approach modeling as design: understand the real need, explore options, and experiment fast — and with AI you can now try new methods, build applications and test them at a speed that was impossible before.
  • The Experiment Loop — Play with a hypothesis: hypothesize → experiment → learn → decide. The mechanism that turns tinkering into a proven block.
  • Fail Fast — Set experiments up to fail quickly and cheaply, read the signal honestly, and move on — don't linger in something that, in the end, may not work.
